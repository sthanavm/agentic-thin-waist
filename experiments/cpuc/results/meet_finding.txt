Google Meet held reference-quality video (720p at 24 fps, freezes under 1%) down to 3 Mb/s symmetric, 8x below IQB's 25 Mb/s conferencing minimum. At 25, 6, 3 Mb/s a speed test puts the link below IQB's minimum while the call is still reference quality, which is the contrast the argument rests on. Meet was demand-limited at 100, 25 Mb/s, where its peak stayed well under the cap, but capacity-limited at 6, 3, 1 Mb/s, where its peak reached the cap and it still delivered reference quality at 6, 3 Mb/s. Quality breaks between 3 and 1 Mb/s: at 1 Mb/s the call fell to 360p at 20 fps with 18% of the session frozen, which is Unusable by the pre-registered thresholds. At 0.5 Mb/s no remote video arrived at all, in two attempts, so the call did not carry video rather than carrying it badly. Across latency, at 20 ms it held reference quality to 3 Mb/s and failed by 1 Mb/s; at 50 ms it held reference quality to 6 Mb/s and failed by 1 Mb/s; at 100 ms it held reference quality to 6 Mb/s. Every latency was measured on the same capacities, so the comparison is like for like.
